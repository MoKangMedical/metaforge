Oncology

PD-1 Inhibitors in Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer

📋 Research Question (PICO)

Population
Adults with advanced NSCLC (Stage IIIB/IV)
Intervention
PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors (nivolumab, pembrolizumab, atezolizumab)
Comparator
Docetaxel or platinum-based chemotherapy
Outcome
Overall survival (OS) and progression-free survival (PFS)

🔍 Search Strategy

Databases: PubMed, Embase, Cochrane Library, ASCO/ESMO abstracts

Search terms: ("non-small cell lung cancer" OR "NSCLC") AND ("PD-1" OR "PD-L1" OR "nivolumab" OR "pembrolizumab" OR "atezolizumab") AND ("randomized controlled trial")

Date range: January 2015 – December 2024

Records identified: 1,247 → 89 after screening → 12 included RCTs

📐 Inclusion / Exclusion Criteria

✓ Inclusion

  • Phase II/III RCTs
  • Stage IIIB/IV NSCLC
  • PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitor monotherapy vs. chemotherapy
  • Reported OS or PFS with HR and 95% CI

✗ Exclusion

  • Combination immunotherapy arms
  • Small cell lung cancer
  • Single-arm studies
  • Non-English publications without extractable data

🏥 Key Findings & Clinical Implications

30% reduction in mortality: PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors significantly improve overall survival compared to docetaxel in second-line advanced NSCLC (pooled HR 0.70, p<0.0001).
PFS benefit with caveats: PFS improvement was significant but more modest (HR 0.76), likely reflecting pseudoprogression and immune-related response patterns.
PD-L1 expression matters: KEYNOTE-024 demonstrated dramatic benefit in PD-L1 ≥50% tumors (OS HR 0.63), supporting biomarker-driven patient selection.
Heterogeneity in PFS: Substantial variability across trials (I²=58%) suggests differences in patient selection, drug mechanism, and endpoint definitions warrant careful interpretation.

Cardiology

SGLT2 Inhibitors in Heart Failure

📋 Research Question (PICO)

Population
Adults with heart failure (HFrEF and HFpEF)
Intervention
SGLT2 inhibitors (dapagliflozin, empagliflozin, sotagliflozin)
Comparator
Matching placebo on standard HF therapy
Outcome
Composite of CV death or HF hospitalization

🔍 Search Strategy

Databases: PubMed, Embase, Cochrane CENTRAL, ClinicalTrials.gov

Search terms: ("heart failure" OR "HFrEF" OR "HFpEF") AND ("SGLT2" OR "sodium-glucose cotransporter 2" OR "dapagliflozin" OR "empagliflozin" OR "sotagliflozin") AND ("randomized")

Date range: January 2015 – December 2024

Records identified: 892 → 64 after screening → 6 included RCTs

📐 Inclusion / Exclusion Criteria

✓ Inclusion

  • Phase III RCTs
  • Diagnosed HF (NYHA II–IV) with LVEF reported
  • SGLT2i vs. placebo + standard care
  • ≥6 months follow-up
  • Reported composite CV outcome with HR

✗ Exclusion

  • Type 2 diabetes prevention studies
  • Acute HF trials (in-hospital only)
  • Dual SGLT1/2 inhibitor crossover designs
  • Abstracts without full data extraction

🏥 Key Findings & Clinical Implications

Class effect confirmed: SGLT2 inhibitors reduce the composite of CV death or HF hospitalization by 24% across the spectrum of HF, regardless of diabetes status (pooled HR 0.76, p<0.00001).
HFrEF and HFpEF benefit: DELIVER and EMPEROR-Preserved closed the long-standing evidence gap for HFpEF — a condition with few proven therapies.
Remarkably consistent results: Very low heterogeneity (I²=8%) across diverse populations, geographic regions, and agents supports a true class effect.
Diabetes-independent: DAPA-HF showed identical benefits in patients with and without type 2 diabetes, establishing SGLT2i as foundational HF therapy.

Neurology

Anti-amyloid Therapy in Alzheimer's Disease

📋 Research Question (PICO)

Population
Adults with early Alzheimer's disease (MCI or mild AD)
Intervention
Anti-amyloid monoclonal antibodies (lecanemab, aducanumab, donanemab)
Comparator
Matching placebo (IV infusion schedule)
Outcome
CDR-SB change from baseline (cognitive decline), ARIA-E incidence

🔍 Search Strategy

Databases: PubMed, Embase, Cochrane Library, ClinicalTrials.gov, FDA documents

Search terms: ("Alzheimer's disease" OR "amyloid") AND ("lecanemab" OR "aducanumab" OR "donanemab" OR "anti-amyloid" OR "monoclonal antibody") AND ("randomized controlled trial")

Date range: January 2015 – December 2024

Records identified: 534 → 31 after screening → 4 included RCTs

📐 Inclusion / Exclusion Criteria

✓ Inclusion

  • Phase III RCTs
  • Confirmed amyloid pathology (PET or CSF)
  • Early AD (MCI due to AD or mild AD dementia)
  • ≥12 months treatment duration
  • CDR-SB as primary or co-primary endpoint

✗ Exclusion

  • Moderate-to-severe AD trials
  • Non-amyloid mechanism of action
  • Single-arm or open-label extensions only
  • Phase I/II dose-finding studies

🏥 Key Findings & Clinical Implications

Proof of concept: Anti-amyloid therapy produces a statistically significant slowing of cognitive decline (pooled Δ CDR-SB = −0.41, p<0.0001), validating the amyloid hypothesis for the first time.
Clinical significance debated: The 22% average slowing translates to ~4.4 months delay in disease progression over 18 months. Whether this is perceptible to patients remains controversial.
Safety signal — ARIA: Amyloid-related imaging abnormalities (ARIA-E) occurred in up to 35% of patients on aducanumab. Lecanemab had a more favorable safety profile (12.6%), but MRI monitoring remains mandatory.
High heterogeneity: I²=71% reflects differences in drug potency, target epitopes, patient selection (amyloid burden, APOE4 status), and the negative GRADUATE trial pulling the estimate toward null.
FDA-approved options: Lecanemab (Leqembi) and donanemab (Kisunla) are now FDA-approved for early AD, but access remains limited by cost (~$26,500/year), infusion requirements, and mandatory MRI monitoring.
